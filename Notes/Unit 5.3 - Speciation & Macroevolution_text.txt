How New Species Evolve¨Evolutionary Species Concept¤The evolutionary species concept distinguishes species from one another based on morphological (structural) traits¤Critical traits for distinguishing species are called diagnostic traits¤Members of a species must share the same, distinct, evolutionary pathway

How New Species Evolve¨Phylogenetic Species Concept¤Relies on the identification of species based on common ancestry¤Evolutionary “family tree”, or phylogenetic tree used¤A branch that contains all descendants of a common ancestor is known as monophyletic–criterion used for defining a species¤Advantage is it relies on DNA sequence, and not morphological traits

How New Species Evolve¨Biological Species Concept¤Populations of the same species breed only among themselves¤Experience reproductive isolation from other such populations¤Often cannot be tested, because very few species in nature overlap in their distribution to even observe reproductive isolation

Modes of Speciation¨Researchers recognize 2 modes of speciation¤One requires populations to be physically isolated from one another, and the other does not. ¨Allopatric Speciation¤Two geographically isolated populations of one species become different species over time¤Can be due to differing selection pressures in differing environments¤Reinforcement occurs when two populations come back together and not longer reproduce because they no longer recognize each other as mates due to changes

Modes of Speciation¨Sympatric Speciation¤One population develops into two or more reproductively isolated groups -No prior geographic isolation¤In plants, sympatric speciation often involves polyploidy(more than the diploid [2n] number)¤A polyploid plant can reproduce with itself, but cannot reproduce with the 2n population because not all the chromosomes would be able to pair during meiosis.nTwo types of polyploidy are known:nAutoploidy -diploid plant produces diploid gametes due to nondisjunction during meiosis.nIf diploid gamete fuses with a haploid gamete, a triploid plant results.nA triploid (3n) plant is sterile and cannot produce offspringnAlloploidy -more complicated process than autoploidynRequires two different but related species of plantsnHybridization is followed by doubling of the chromosomes.

Modes of Speciation¨Adaptive Radiation¤Occurs when members of a species invade several new geographically separate environments¤The populations become adapted to the different environments¤Many new species evolve from the single ancestral speciesnDarwin’s finches are an example

Modes of Speciation¨Convergent Evolution¤Occurs when a similar biological trait evolves in two unrelated species as a result of exposure to similar environments.¤Traits evolving in this manner are termed analogous traits.¤Similar function, but different originnbird wing vs. bat wingnChichlidsin 2 African lakesnMore closely related to species in own lake than morphological similar from other lake

Principles of Macroevolution¨Macroevolution¤Evolution at the species or higher level of classification¤Some evolutionists support a gradualisticmodelnEvolution at the species level occurs graduallynSpeciation occurs after populations become isolatednEach group continues its own evolutionary pathwaynThe gradualisticmodel suggests that it is difficult to indicate when speciation occurred¤Some paleontologists support the punctuated equilibrium modelnSpecies can appear quite suddenlynSpecies then remain essentially unchanged phenotypically during a period of stasis (sameness) until they undergo extinction.nThis model states that periods of equilibrium are punctuated by speciation
